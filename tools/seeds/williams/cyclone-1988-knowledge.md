# Cyclone (Williams, 1988)

Coverage: **partial - every controller address enumerated and named; wiring, polarity, mechanisms, the three ROM
revisions and recreation behaviour validated; the switches, coils, three flashers and the playfield G.I. are placed from
the retained table, but spatial placement stays open because no retained table models a lamp bulb and the placements
are not yet checked against the factory drawings.**

The definition is generated by `tools/curate_cyclone.py` from `tools/cyclone_data.py`; the spatial review is
`reports/spatial/williams/cyclone-1988.md`, the runtime evidence is derived by `tools/cyclone_runtime_evidence.py`, and
the ROM's service-name tables are decoded by `tools/cyclone_rom_name_tables.py`.

## Identity and variants

CYCLONE is Williams model 564 (IPDB 617, OPDB `G4O2b-MJpn4`), a System 11B game produced from February 24 to August 11,
1988 (9,408 units per IPDB), with design by Barry Oursler, art by Python Anghelo, mechanics by Joe Joos Jr., music by Chris Granner and
software by Bill Pfutzenreuter. The Instruction Manual is 16-564-101 (February 1988) and the Parts Information Manual
16-564-102 (March 1988).

The pinned catalog has three drivers, all selecting the same game data (`GEN_S11B`, `s11_dispS11b1`, A/C relay 12,
`FLIP_SWNO(57,58)`) and the same sound ROMs: `cycln_l5` (root, the known-working script's ROM), `cycln_l4` and
`cycln_l1`. Only the U26 and U27 game ROMs differ. The three U27 name tables decode to identical text at identical
offsets, and the L-4 and L-1 Coil Tests step the same 28 pulses under the same names as L-5, so every driver is the same
physical machine.

Domestic machines differ only in field modifications from 1988 service bulletins: SS49 (July 25) adds a red post and a
rubber ring by the "Ken's Vending" plastic so balls cannot stick between the plastic and the Cyclone ramp, and SS52
(October 13) adds a support bracket under the end of the Spookhouse ramp. Neither changes the controller contract.
IPDB also shows a Japanese-market game, coined for yen, whose backbox has a spinning light show instead of the
mechanical Mystery Wheel, in unknown numbers. No source here says which ROM it ran or how its backbox is wired, so this
record does not cover it.

## Controller facts that matter to a recreation

- **Matrices.** Switches and lamps are System 11's sequential 1-64 (column `(n-1)/8+1`, row `(n-1)%8+1`). The switch
  matrix is read uninverted, and the ROM's Switch Levels test names every fitted switch at public 1, so every matrix
  contact, the two optos included, rests open. Unfitted switch positions: 2, 12, 33, 38-40 and 42-52. The ROM still
  names 2 "G. SCORE BOARD", which no Cyclone source describes. The centre coin chute (5) is not fitted on US games.
- **A/C select relay at 12.** The eight switched drivers serve an "A" load at 1-8 while 12 is released and a "C" load at
  25-32 while it is energized. 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7 are coils; 2, 6 and 8 carry no load; all eight C loads are flasher
  circuits, most with bulbs both on the playfield and in the backbox. No switch mirrors the relay.
- **Special solenoids 17-21** (left jet, left kicker, right jet, right kicker, bottom jet) are fired by the ROM itself:
  the driver maps no special-solenoid switch, so a consumer drives only the switches 60-64 and the ROM answers with the
  coil while the enable 23 is on. 22 is unused.
- **Game-on 23** rises at Start, dropped at the playfield tilt (9) in the gameplay run, and rose again for the next
  ball; the manual tilts the ball in play at the playfield tilt or the third plumb-bob closure. The retained script binds it to its FastFlips helper.
- **Flippers.** `FLIP_SWNO(57,58)`: drive the cabinet buttons at 84 (left) and 82 (right). PinMAME copies them into the
  lane-change switches 57 and 58 on every update and fabricates 45-48; a single write to 57 or 58 is overwritten.
- **Mystery Wheel stepper 13 and 14.** They are logic inputs to the stepper control board, not pulsed coils (see below).
- **G.I.** Two relays, PinMAME-typed as reverse 6.3 V AC G.I. outputs: 10 turns the playfield G.I. off when energized
  and 11 the backglass G.I.
- **Ferris wheel motor 16** energizes a relay that switches the motor; the matrix has no Ferris wheel position switch.
- **Country jumper.** DIP 0 reaches the CPU as `core_getDip(0)<<7` through an eight-bit handler, so only bit 0 matters.
  The retained script's `.DIP(0) = &H80` therefore reads as 0, the same as every harness run.
- **Displays.** Player 1 and 2 are seven 16-segment characters each (indices 0 and 1, segment starts 1 and 9), player 3
  and 4 seven 7-segment digits (indices 2 and 3, starts 21 and 29). The service tests print names on players 1 and 2
  and test numbers on player 3. The ROM's font draws 0 and O, and 5 and S, with the same segments.
- **Power-up.** Every controlled output 9-16 shows one 50 ms pulse about 0.26 s after power-up, before the ROM sets up
  the PIA; then the ROM fires 4 and 3 once (2.55 and 2.68 s) and runs the Ferris wheel motor for about 9.4 s.

## Mechanisms and behaviour

- **Outhole and serve.** There is no ball trough switch or ball-release coil: a drained ball rests on the outhole
  switch (10) and the outhole kicker (1) serves it to the shooter lane (25). At Start the ROM kicked 0.33 s later and
  again 2.09 s after that while 10 stayed closed; after a drain it waited for the bonus count (6.2 s). With 10 held at power-up and
  no game, it never kicked.
- **Shooter channel skill shot.** The plunged ball climbs the elevated shooter channel past the Shuttle switches 30 (5K,
  topmost), 29 (25K), 28 (100K) and 27 (25K, lower) and drops into the Shuttle kickbig (26); the Ball Shooter Kickbig (3,
  AE-24-900) kicks it into play about 1.1 s later.
- **Spook House.** A single centre drop target (18, an opto assembly) guards a cellar hole that, in IPDB's words,
  feeds the scoop; the Score Spook House switch (11) sits behind the target, and bulletin SS52 places the Spookhouse
  ramp under the playfield below the Spookhouse entrance hole. The Boomerang Kickbig (13) holds the ball until its coil
  (4, AE-26-1500) fires it round the Boomerang Loop, lit by three #1251 lamps (15). With the target held down the ROM
  flashed 15 eight times, pulsed the drop-target reset (5) twice and fired 4 5.25 s after 13 closed, then pulsed 5
  twice more; it pulses 5 again at the start of each ball. Closing 11 also drove the Mystery Wheel stepper (13 and 14
  on together for 0.72 s).
- **Ferris wheel.** A playfield wheel (C-11900) at the top left, turned through a belt and pulley by a motor
  (14-7941-3) that relay 16 switches; the ball enters past the Ferris Wheel Entrance switch (17), a ball-support bar
  sits under the wheel (SS50), and the wheel carries the ball for part of its rotation (IPDB). The ROM runs the motor at Start and keeps it running while switches keep closing:
  it stopped 9.5 s after the last switch and restarted with the next. The retained table models the wheel as a rotating
  primitive that carries a ball round on an arc; how the physical wheel holds and releases the ball is not printed.
- **Mystery Wheel.** A backbox carousel (C-12036) with sixteen lamps 33-48 round it at 22.5-degree steps clockwise from
  top dead centre, turned by a two-winding stepper motor. Its control board (D-12045, with subassembly D-12046) takes
  the CPU's outputs 13 and 14 as two logic inputs, each steering one winding through a transistor bridge, and carries
  the home opto that reports at switch 41 through a transistor across the matrix row and column. The ROM spins the wheel
  by changing the levels of 13 and 14; in the Mystery Wheel Test it held both on and dropped each briefly in turn while
  stepping, and counted to 256 and reported errors with 41 held at either level. With 41 at 0 the test shows "H" on the player 4 display,
  which the manual reads as the home switch open or the opto interrupted; a rising edge of 41 restarts the step count.
  The retained script models it as a cvpmMech stepper with `.Length = 200` and `.Steps = 360`: pinned mech.c wraps the
  position at the length and reports it on a 360-position scale, and the script closes 41 over reported positions 0-180,
  half a turn. It names 14 first and 13 second, but its two-step mech moves whenever either input changes, so the order
  sets neither the windings' letters nor the direction. The physical home flag's width and the motor's steps per turn
  are not printed.
- **Jet bumpers and kickers.** Three jets (17, 19, 21; AE-23-800 coils) and two slingshot kickers (18, 20; AE-26-1500)
  fired by the ROM from their switches 60-64.
- **Ramps and targets.** The clear Comet ramp (entry 31, score 32, RIDE THE COMET lamp 8) and Cyclone ramp (score 34);
  the COMET value lamps 27-32 stand on the panel above the rear rail. Three Duck targets (19-21), three Ball Toss targets
  (22-24) and the Spot Ball Toss target (59); top lanes 1-2-3 (14-16); outlanes (53, 54) and return lanes (55, 56);
  three 10-point rubber switches on the right (35-37).

## Manual notes

- The Solenoid Table names 09 the Spook House Bonus Flasher; the Solenoids/Flashers list and the ROM's Coil Test name it
  the Spins Mystery Wheel flasher, which the definition follows.
- The Solenoid Table prints the stepper outputs 13 as Coil B and 14 as Coil A, the Solenoids/Flashers list the other way
  round; the definition follows the Solenoid Table, which the retained script's stepper model agrees with.
- The Solenoid Table prints 20 (Right Kicker) as AE-23-800 and 21 (Bottom Jet Bumper) as AE-26-1500; the Solenoids/
  Flashers list and the parts manual's kicker and jet bumper assemblies give 20 AE-26-1500 and 21 AE-23-800, which the
  definition follows.
- The Switches list footnotes the Mystery Wheel opto as part of D-12046; the parts manual lists it on D-12045, and
  bulletin SS47 lists D-12046 as a subassembly of D-12045.
- The Test/Diagnostic page 36 calls Switch Edges "06" where page 35 prints 07.
- The retained script's `sw59_Hit` pulses 25 instead of 59, so its Spot Ball Toss target never reaches the ROM; its
  comments call 25 and 26 "pops".

## Placement notes

- Switches are placed at the triggers, kickers, targets, rubbers, bumpers, slingshot walls and flippers the script binds.
  The lane-change switches 57 and 58 are placed at the flipper pivots their drawing balloons point at.
- Coils are placed at their kickers, bumpers and slingshot walls; the drop-target reset at the target, the boomerang
  kickbig coil at its kicker and the Ferris wheel motor at the wheel.
- Flashers 29, 30 and 31 are placed at one table light each that sits under a modelled flasher dome. The table lights
  15, 25, 26, 28, 32 and the other playfield bulbs of 29 and 30 through lights with no dome or bulb, and 27's dome stands
  on the back panel above the playfield, so they take no placement; 9 has no table light at all.
- The playfield G.I. (10) is placed at the 32 lights of the table's GI collection that model a bulb.
- No retained table models an insert bulb, so no playfield lamp is placed.
