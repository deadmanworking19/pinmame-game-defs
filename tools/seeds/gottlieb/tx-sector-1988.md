## Physical identity and firmware

IPDB 2699 and Pinside 517 independently identify TX-Sector, March 1988, Premier Technology (Gottlieb), model 712, System 80B. The six pinned drivers are `txsector`, French `txsectrf`, German `txsectrg`, and their Flipprojets free-play counterparts `txsectfp`, `txsecffp`, `txsecgfp`. Each independently declares the same generation, two 20-character alphanumeric rows, sound board, zero inversion mask and routing. Flipprojets documents a replacement 8 KB PROM 1 on the original piggyback with no board modification; all six are physically identical firmware variants. Only the parent ROM archive is available locally and runtime-tested; no clone execution is claimed. Language and credit behavior differ, not playfield hardware.

IPDB documents Vitrigraph and silkscreened playfield artwork variations. No documented difference in switches, mechanisms or controller topology was found; artwork shades do not justify another machine record. Manual replacement-display notices describe board revisions, not an extra physical display. Two backbox VFD rows are inventory; an extra emulator callback is not another fitted display.

## Controller contract

The scoped [System 80B profile](../../controllers/pinmame/gts80b.json) follows PinMAME `46e1a01a0f73dc5ce163d98db1ae435883ecc450`. Public switch numbers use decimal strobes/returns: `00-77` with final digit `0-7`. Digits `8/9` do not identify cells. Dedicated inputs and the synthetic `111-118` column are separate. Keyboard handling rewrites `07, 17, 27, 37, 47, 57` only when enabled; the retained table and evidentiary runs leave it off.

TX-Sector's six game declarations select `invSw = 0`, `FLIP_SWNO(6,16)` with no `FLIP_SOL` or EOS flags, `hw.lampCol = 0`, and therefore `nLamps = 64`. They select `SNDBRD_GTS80B` and module `gts80bs2`. Those are game facts rather than inherited System 80B defaults. For this unmasked matrix public `1` is the CPU's set/closed bit; dedicated slam public `1` pulls the RIOT U5 input low and represents the open/slammed physical NC contact. The selected sound module reads its Y-CPU input at `0x4000`: settings `37-40` feed bits `0-3`, settings `41-42` feed bits `4-5`, setting `33` is inverted at bit `6`, and speech DRQ is bit `7`. Settings `34-36` do not feed that read. The physical fitment and TX sound-ROM meanings of the reused sound settings remain unresolved.

Use `114` for the left cabinet button, `112` for the right. PinMAME copies them into `06` and `16` every update, where the physical cabinet diagram has advance contacts. Direct writes to `06/16` are overwritten. The old table's extra writes are redundant because sys80.vbs also writes the correct buttons. The synthetic `45/46` pair follows the right button; `47/48` follows the left while game-on. These are button-state mirrors rather than measurements of independent windings or EOS timing. A matrix level of `1` represents a closed contact; dedicated slam-tilt `1` is the open/slammed state of a normally closed physical contact. Consume public state without applying the informational controller inversion flag again.

Solenoids `1-9` are real drivers. Output `10` is synthetic game-on from lamp `0`, independent of lamp `1` on System 80B; `11` mirrors tilt. Addresses `12-44` and `49-50` have no TX-specific writer, and custom outputs `51+` are absent. These unpublished ranges are explicitly accounted for. Lamps `0-47` are latched outputs. A selector-12 write publishes binary `48-51` as complements of `44-47`; this is stored latch behavior, not an unconditional initialization or physical-brightness identity. Physical-mode lamps `52-60` mirror solenoids `1-9`. Out-of-range selectors can write binary lamps `48-59`, so a quiet trace never proves those addresses unused. Lamps `61-63` have no writer. GI `0` carries a reversed tilt-relay model in physical mode and stays `0` in binary mode. The table toggles visual GI from lamp `1` with `UseGI = 0`.

The factory prints Q as `L3` on its playboard sheet and assignment note while also assigning Shoot Again to `L3`. Driver-board routing, script and service test distinguish Q/public 0 from Shoot Again/public 3. Both printed readings remain in the excerpts. The discrepancy is a number on the wiring document, not two physical devices sharing one software binding. The game-specific PDF 48 table clearly prints L21 at A3J3-22 (wire 322), whereas the board provides Q22/public 21 at pin 20 and Q15/public 14 at pin 22. Its L43 pin glyph (wire 744) is E or 2; ambiguous, while the board provides Q44/public 43 at pin 2 and Q49/public 48 at pin E. These literal readings are disclosed on devices 14/21/43/48 as wiring-detail observations. Structured 14/48 paths identify board provisions only; harness pins for lamps 21/43 stay unset because logical runtime bindings cannot independently prove physical connector wiring.

## Ball transport and mechanisms

Three modeled balls start with trough `56` and outhole `66` asserted. Outhole driver `9` moves a drained ball through the return/trough; lamp 2 drives the separate release coil into the shooter lane. The manual plunger launches along the elevated wireform, through rollunder `63`, to the upper-left-flipper area. The script deletes/creates balls and tracks TroughBalls as a simulation convenience. A recreation must preserve occupied physical sensors during travel and captures, not move them directly because a nearby coil fired. The exact trough-contact coordinate is not yet established.

Four holes have individual occupied sensors `45/55/65/75` and plunger/link/cam/spring kickers. Three assemblies are MA-1049 and one MA-1061. Upper holes score teleport awards; lower holes can retain a ball and initiate the opposite teleport. Maximum energy permits capture; the shooter ramp activates multiball with two balls captured. The game can run two-ball multiball while having a larger total installed inventory.

S multiplexes driver `1` between the top-left hole and two left teleport bulbs, `2` between the lower-left hole and two right teleport bulbs, `5` between the top-right hole and two left-ramp bulbs, and `6` between the lower-right hole and two right-ramp bulbs. A19 uses steering diodes and each paired #67 circuit has a 4-ohm resistor. Preserve all eight bulbs. The printed table labels physical S ON as hole and OFF as flash. Both exact scripts select holes at public `12 = 0` and flash effects at `12 = 1`; that runtime predicate is settled. The retained label distinction is recorded as **ignored**, because physical armature energization cannot change the recreation's public branch selection. No physical energization sense is inferred.

The [complete state derivation](../../evidence/excerpts/gottlieb.tx-sector.1988/s-relay-polarity.md) distinguishes all supplied hole inputs from native ROM output callbacks. The first six shared-driver pulses have no preceding host hole assertion and public `12 = 1`. Native zero-level intervals of 40.710568-40.928613 s and 43.074963-43.333056 s contain shared solenoid-5/2 rises respectively. The host had already released 65/75 before those callbacks, so these times do not show a coil releasing a sensor, or a direct sensor-75-to-coil-2 connection. Callback times are host observations, not factory latency. Host input/readback is not independent ROM or physical occupancy evidence. The script predicates and native levels together support the public routing under the synthetic stimulus; no additional ROM probe is deferred.

Two four-target banks latch down and reset together through outputs `4` (top) and `3` (right). Keep `40/50/60/70` or `41/51/61/71` closed while each target remains down; release after reset. Bank completion can reset the opposite bank. Rollover drop target `42` has a distinct trip coil (lamp `13`) and reset coil (solenoid `7`); right-return behavior can trip it. A target jam should keep its actual sensor asserted; no retry period is asserted without evidence.

Three pop bumpers fire locally through their own boards and 5194 coils. Two contact kickers/slings and two kicking targets use 1496 coils under Q supply. Their actuation contacts are independent of the scoring matrix, and the seven scoring/event regions share input `76`. The manual's adjustment drawing separates a target's actuating blade and scoring switch. Represent the local mechanisms individually without inventing CPU outputs. Two spinners have individual pulse inputs `73/74`. Passive gates and ramp rollunders have no evidenced motor drive.

Two left flippers and one right use local 20095 coils and cabinet buttons, enabled through Q. Physical EOS hardware is local. Virtual power/hold mirrors do not identify independent hardware windings. Q/T/S states and the controlled-lamp supply are not interchangeable; the emulator does not model every supply contact. Three #455 energy-level bulbs, three coin-chute bulbs and lightbox illumination have no additional controlled address in this record.

Script speeds, exit angles, timer values, walls and animation delays are table tuning. The script's 200-degree and 205-degree exit angles are recreation clues rather than factory measurements. Startup bank resets, ball searches and release pulses are ROM-driven; fresh state, initial sensors and output mode are recorded in the harness evidence. The exploratory capture sequence is a synthetic sensor probe with no claim of full rules qualification or measured jam duration.

## Spatial evidence and limits

The original table bounds are `(0, 0)-(1000, 2000)`, with x left-to-right and y rear-to-apron. Normalize once and round to six decimals. Exact object JSON digests and script bindings are in the spatial excerpt. Seven independent manual feature reads span more than half the drawing height; maximum leave-one-out error is 0.024 normalized. All candidate coordinates remain observed. Rear/perimeter lamp checks lie outside the control hull, so the fit does not validate them.

Each of lamps `15-19` has three factory #86 sockets; mesh-bearing `L15/L15A/L15B` and their counterparts supply those candidates. C-series lights are glows.
