# The Lord of the Rings Limited Edition (Stern, 2009)

Coverage: **author_ready - the 2003 machine's playfield, matrices, mechanisms and 216 validated placements, carried to this edition because its own cabinet chart prints the same switch, lamp and coil tables and the same playfield maps, with the edition's own wiring, its right Helm's Deep flasher moved to driver 28, its optional shaker motor and its 10.02 firmware.** The 2003 production machine's record, `stern.lord-of-the-rings.2003`, holds the evidence the shared parts rest on, and its knowledge note explains it in full; the sections at the end of this note are copied from it.

## Identity

IPDB 5558 (`ipdb-5558` excerpt) lists The Lord of the Rings (Limited Edition) as a Stern game of December 2009, model number I-00B4, on a "Stern Whitestar (modified)" MPU, about 500 units, and calls it the "Limited Edition version of Stern's 2003 'The Lord of the Rings'". OPDB's record `GrqZX-MDEPd`, "Lord of the Rings (LE)", carries the same IPDB number; the 2003 machine is `GrqZX-MD15w`. The LE owner whose documents this record rests on gives the same model number and "LE first made: Dec 2009". The year here is the physical build year. PinMAME's catalog dates the `lotr_le` firmware 2008, and that date stays on the driver.

PinMAME has one driver for this edition, `lotr_le` (10.02). It is declared a clone of `lotr` and shares `init_lotr`, so the emulator routes it exactly like the 2003 firmware. Against that machine's root driver it changes only the CPU game ROM, `lotrcpua.a02` for `lotrcpua.a00`, and keeps the display ROM `lotrdspa.a00` and the `LOTR_SND` set (`src/wpc/segames.c:1852-1858`). A string search of both ROM sets finds the service menus' switch, lamp and coil names in that shared display ROM and none in either CPU ROM; its coil table spells each number in letters from `a` for 0, so `#be FLASH:HELMS DEEP RT` is 14 and `#ci NOT USED` is 28. 10.02 therefore names every address exactly as 10.00 does; the names cannot tell the editions apart and are evidence for neither.

## Why this is its own record

Shared routing does not make shared hardware, and three independent kinds of evidence say the hardware changed:

- **The factory list.** IPDB's list of factory changes includes "Game has the option to add a Shaker Motor", which the 2003 game never had, and quotes Lonnie Ropp's confirmation that the shaker code was added to the original code and secured with a new PAL chip version.
- **An owner's first-hand documents.** Richard Harvey ("firepower") documented his new-in-box Limited Edition on a blog and a documentation page: a report that the cabinet chart Stern stapled into the LE was the 2003 one and that "wire colors and 2 solenoids positions were changed"; a corrected LE cabinet chart; the manual's Section 5 pages 100-106 annotated with his machine's wiring; and a list of the edition's changes, including "520-5249-00 SAM I/O Power Driver used (instead of 520-5137-01 White Star)" and "Helms Deep Rt. (Flash Lamp) moved from Sol #14 to Sol #28".
- **The firmware.** 10.02 drives the right Helm's Deep flasher on 14 and 28 together, 10.00 on 14 alone. Hash-pinned runs of each ROM's own flash-lamp test (`evidence/runtime/whitestar/lord-of-the-rings-flash-test.json`, `evidence/runtime/whitestar/lord-of-the-rings-limited-edition-flash-test.json`) and of a served and played game (`evidence/runtime/whitestar/lord-of-the-rings-limited-edition-gameplay.json`) show 10.02 switching 28 on and off together with 14 every time, each pair of solenoid callbacks reaching the harness within 1 ms of each other, and 10.00 never touching 28.

## The Limited Edition evidence

No factory Limited Edition document is available. The owner says Stern supplied the LE with a manual hand-edited from the 2003 one, "with whiteout and a photocopier", still with mistakes and missing pages 100-106; his own draft of a complete LE manual, his LE quick reference and Stern's shaker kit instructions are no longer online and were never archived. What is retained:

- **The LE cabinet chart** (`cabinet-chart` excerpt, structured as `tools/seeds/stern/lord-of-the-rings-limited-edition-2009-chart.json`). Stern's 11x17 Lord of the Rings cabinet chart (SPI Nr. 755-5310-80) as the owner corrected it for his LE and published it "to go inside LE lower cabinet". It is the edition's only complete wiring table, so the structured wiring follows it. Its switch matrix, dedicated switches and lamp matrix match the 2003 manual's cell for cell - every name, location, switch part number and bulb - apart from four printing differences: it spells lamps 15 and 33 LEGOLAS where the 2003 scan prints LEGOLES, prints lamp 5's number as `4`, its green POTD bulbs read `Grn.` where the 2003 transcription read `Gm.`, and it leaves DS-3's location cell blank where the 2003 manual prints `on Cabinet Side`. Its coil table matches too, except rows #12, #14 and #28 and the wire colours. The chart also carries the switch, coil and lamp location maps of the playfield.
- **The annotated manual pages** (`owner-annotated-manual-pages` excerpt). The scans are the 2003 manual's own Section 5 pages - page 100 matches Stern's reissue of the 2003 manual cell for cell - so only the owner's overlays describe the LE. They agree with the chart on rows 14 and 28, the 520-5249-00 board and the lamp wiring (page 100 still prints 12 NOT USED; the shaker on 12 rests on the chart, the owner's notes and IPDB's change list), and disagree with it on several wire colours and on which J10 pins feed the flippers (see below).
- **The owner's documentation, knocker and shaker pages** (`owner-documentation-pages` excerpt).
- **Stern's 520-5249-00 I/O power driver board schematic** (`io-power-driver-520-5249-00` excerpt): the board carries the same driver designators the chart prints, Q1-Q32 for the solenoids, Q33-Q42 for the lamp rows and U10-U17 for the lamp columns.
- **Stern service bulletin 148** (`service-bulletin-148` excerpt), for the Balrog LED module.

The owner's documents are one person's account of one machine, and he says himself that Stern may have used different wire colours on other LEs. They agree with each other on everything a recreation consumes, and with IPDB's factory list and the firmware on the changed drivers.

## What carries over from the 2003 machine, and why

Every switch, lamp, coil, flasher, the G.I. channel, the display, all 8 mechanisms, the 3 relationships and all 216 placements come from the 2003 record, with their evidence: the 2003 manual and its 2022 reissue, three retained recreations of the 2003 machine, the factory drawings the placements were checked on, and harness runs of the 2003 firmware 10.00 (the stacking opto and the 19-LED board). That evidence was gathered on the 2003 machine. It holds for the LE because:

- the LE chart prints the same switch matrix, dedicated switches, lamp matrix and coil set, cell for cell but for the printing differences above, and the same switch, coil and lamp location maps; the curator (`tools/curate_lord_of_the_rings_le.py`) refuses to build if any other switch, dedicated-switch or lamp cell, connector, driver or IC differs from the 2003 tables;
- the owner's annotated pages keep the 2003 under-playfield drawing (page 105) and the switch and lamp wiring diagrams (page 104) with every name, changing only wire colours;
- what the owner lists as the LE's playfield changes leaves every device where it was: the Arwen playfield plastic returns, the plastics have no mounting holes and carry no Armies of Middle Earth figures, the Balrog has the LED upgrade, the centre ramp has a post upgrade, and the playfield is signed and extra-clearcoated. The rest is cabinet trim: plated and polished lockdown bar, legs, hinges and side rails, brass bolts, gold trim, a gold-effect real-glass backglass and the 500 Game Limited Edition plate.

Each carried device and placement cites the LE chart (and placements the annotated pages) beside its 2003 evidence, and each device note keeps the 2003 wording, which names the 2003 documents.

## Edition differences a recreation needs

- **Right Helm's Deep flasher on 28.** Bind it to public solenoid 28. The LE chart prints #28 FLASH: HELMS DEEP RT on Q28, control line BLU-BLK at J6-P4, power YEL-ORG at J6-P10, 20v DC; the 2003 machine has it on 14.
- **Solenoid 14 is unconnected.** The chart prints #14 NOT USED NC J9-P7. 10.02 still drives 14 as an exact mirror of 28, so the state published at 14 is the flasher's, with no load behind it on this edition. Do not bind a second flasher there.
- **Solenoid 12 is the optional shaker motor.** The LE harness brings the BLU-YEL drive line from Q12 (J9-P5) to a single-wire connector at the back of the cabinet, and a RED/WHT supply connector near the plumb-bob tilt, for Stern's Rev B shaker kit, which brings its own motor and shaker board and mounts on the cabinet floor. No shaker is fitted from the factory; 10.02 never drove 12 in the gameplay runs at default settings.
- **Optional coil 24** is unchanged; the LE chart names it OPTIONAL COIL (KNOCKER), and the owner fitted a hardware knocker there through adjustment #46 Q24 option.
- **The Balrog flasher (32) is a red LED module.** Service bulletin 148 replaced the initial run's #89 bulb with LED module 112-5023-02 (12.8v, 20-25mA) in socket 077-5003-00-80 from April 2004; the owner lists "Balrog has LED upgrade" for the LE, though the chart still prints #89 Bulb. It is dimmer than the bulb and still driven on 32.
- **Boards.** The LE runs a Stern 520-5249-00 S.A.M. I/O power driver board in place of the 2003 machine's 520-5137-01, fed through a "Z" connector that adapts the old J10 harness plug to the new board; the owner's page 101 relabels the board-side J10 pins 10,9 / 8 / 7,6 where the 2003 page prints 1,2 / 3 / 4,5, while the chart keeps the harness-side J10-P1/2, P3 and P4/5. Fuse and bridge numbers changed with the board, as the owner's page lists; his page 101 relabels the 2003 page's fuses F6 (7A, the 48v AC feed of the 50v bridge) as F5, F20 (the ring magnet's) as F7 4A with "*May need 5A S.B.", F21 (3A) as F8, F7 (the 20v bridge's feed) as F10 and F22 (the controlled-lamp bridge's feed) as F9, and BRIDGE 20 as BRIDGE 4. The owner's page gives the CPU/Sound board as a 520-5300-B4 White Star CPU/Sound Board II with an Atmel processor, his 2010 post as a 520-5300-00 Rev A.

Each affected device's note gives the other reading. None of it changes which device sits on which address.

## Firmware

`lotr_le` is the edition's production firmware, 10.02. Beyond the flasher mirror it behaves like 10.00 in the retained runs: the same trough serve, and the same coils and flashers answering the same switches; the shaker on 12 stays off at default settings. The shaker code and its adjustment were not exercised.

## The 520-5242-00 LED board: nineteen separately addressable emitters

`lotrGameData` declares two board identifiers. The second, `SE_BOARDID_520_5242_00`, is named in `src/wpc/se.h` in plain words: **"Lord of The Rings 19 LED Board"**. `se.c` drives it through `core_set_pwm_output_led_vfd(CORE_MODOUT_LAMP0 + 80, 3 * 8, CORE_MODOUT_LED, 4.f / 2.f)` and writes at `CORE_MODOUT_LAMP0 + 80`, `+ 88` and `+ 96` masked `0x07` - public lamp addresses **81 to 99**, nineteen LEDs. So `lampCol = 5` is not slack: it is 64 base lamps, the two auxiliary matrix columns at 65-80, and room for that board. Allocation slots 100-104 are not physical devices.

The manual names every LED. Its LED Test (PDF 42) cycles ALL ON and LED #1 to #19 and says LED #1 is the board's L1, the rest following suit; its component layout (PDF 162) draws L1 to L19, with the Top Edge marked. A hash-pinned run of the ROM's own LED TEST (`evidence/runtime/whitestar/lord-of-the-rings-led-test.json`) shows ALL ON raising exactly 81-99 and each LED #n raising exactly one of them, every DMD frame pinned by its digest with its reading. The board sits under the round window between the flippers, and the lamp location drawing DR.5 draws its nineteen windows: three in a centre triangle, seven in a middle ring and nine in an outer ring, with a single window at the rear and a pair at the front, exactly the printed layout with its Top Edge to the rear. A similarity fit of the layout to the windows (no reflection; the layout is drawn from the LED side, and both bottom views draw the board's connector J1 on the right) pairs each window with its designator at 0.57 px rms at 300 dpi.

## Addresses that are synthetic, mirrored or reserved

- Public 45 and 47 are the power-phase view of the same physical coils exposed at 46 and 48. Binding both halves creates two flippers where the machine has one.
- Public 15 is PinMAME's synthetic fast-flip and game-on state; 16 is its unused companion.
- Whitestar auxiliary board 520-5068-01 exposes three outputs, so 33, 34 and 35 carry the UK-only up/down posts and 36 is unused.
- General illumination is a **single aggregate channel 0**, although the cabinet has four separately fused circuits. A recreation must not infer four controllable channels from the fuse chart.
- On this edition, public 14 is the 10.02 firmware's mirror of the right Helm's Deep flasher on 28, with nothing connected; 12 is the optional shaker motor.

## Opto polarity: PinMAME normalizes nothing here

The controller profile declares `inversion_applied_by_emulator: true` as a platform capability, and for this driver pinned PinMAME exercises none of it. `lotrGameData` (`segames.c:1498`) is a positional aggregate that sets only `GEN_WS`, the display layout and the `hw` struct; the trailing `wpc` member - which is where `invSw` lives - is left at C zero-initialization, and `core.c:2455` memcpy's those zeros into the live `coreGlobals.invSw`. **The four printed optos are therefore published exactly as a recreation asserts them.** `se.c`'s `switch_r` returns `~core_getSwCol`, so public 1 is what the CPU reads as a closed matrix contact; with the evidence below, that makes the opto boards' matrix-facing contact rest open and close while a ball blocks the beam, and nothing in the firmware compensates for a closed rest state.

All four are settled by observation rather than by argument. The retained known-working VPW 1.6 script asserts switches 14, 41, 47 when a ball is present, through direct assert/release pairs whose sense is unambiguous - and it is known-working on the ball trough, which is exactly where a reversed opto fails first. Switch 15 is bound by no retained recreation, so its sense was asked of the ROM directly. Hash-pinned LibPinMAME runs of `lotr` (`evidence/runtime/whitestar/lord-of-the-rings-stacking-opto.json`) with four balls held on 11-14 show the ROM acting on public 1 and quiet at 0: held at 1 from power-up, or raised to 1 in attract mode, it fires the trough up-kicker (public 1) and, because the harness never lets a ball move, fires it six times in all before firing the auto launch (public 2) once; 0 at boot and a fall back to 0 draw no coil at all. That 1 means a ball blocking the stacking opto is an inference, corroborated by switch 14 on the same 515-0173-00 / 515-0174-00 board pair, which the known-working table asserts at 1 while a ball is present. Hold 15 at 0 at rest, drive it to 1 only while a ball blocks the stacking opto, and never invert it.

`normally_closed` is false on all four: the matrix-facing opto contact rests open, exactly like the mechanical switches. The reissue's Balrog test (PDF 42) says switches 31, 32 and 28 "are in a Normally Closed state", but the same paragraph has each end switch read closed at its own end position, and every retained script holds 28, 31 and 32 at 0 except while the Balrog is hit or parked at that end; the matrix-facing contacts rest open like every other switch here.

## Custom mechanisms

- **Four-ball trough and up-kicker.** Four balls rest in the under-playfield trough on printed switches 11, 12 and 13 with the fourth position read by opto 14, the 4-Ball Trough VUK Opto at the eject end. Solenoid 1, the Trough Up-Kicker, lifts the ball at the eject end into the shooter lane, where switch 16 reads it. Printed switch 15, the 4-Ball Stacking Opto, watches the stack feeding the trough. The manual numbers the positions from the left, so printed Trough #1 is the position furthest from the shooter lane; the retained VPW script numbers its trough objects in the opposite order, which is a labelling difference and not a wiring disagreement. All three retained scripts initialise switches 11-14 closed at start of game because balls rest on them.
- **Balrog motorized toy.** The Balrog toy is driven by motor 041-5088-01 on printed solenoid 22, gated by the DC relay 520-5066-00 on printed solenoid 20. Printed switches 31 Balrog Open and 32 Balrog Closed read the two end positions and are mutually exclusive; the retained VPW script asserts one and clears the other as the motor runs. Printed switch 28 Balrog Hit is a separate above-playfield switch on the toy that registers a ball strike and is driven in the retained script from a wobble value rather than from the motor position, so a recreation must not derive it from the open or closed state.
- **Sword ball lock.** Three above-playfield switches read balls held in the sword lock: printed 17 Sword Lock High, 18 Sword Lock Mid and 19 Sword Lock Low, all switch part 180-5119-02. Printed solenoid 21, the Sword Lock Release, frees the stack. The high position is the one furthest from the release coil.
- **One Ring magnet and back panel.** The One Ring on the back panel is held and released by the magnet on printed solenoid 6, which is the only output with its own fuse, printed F20 4A 250v slow-blow and marked THIS GAME ONLY in the quick reference fuse chart. Printed opto 47 Ring Made, on the back panel, reads a ball completing the ring. Printed solenoid 26 flashes the ring and 27 the back panel behind it.
- **Loop diverter gate.** Printed solenoid 8, the Loop Diverter, steers a ball out of the loop toward the left Orthanc tower. The manual carries a dedicated Diverter Gate Adjustment Procedure: with power off and the playfield raised, the crank-bar set screw is loosened, the paddle is held against the right flat rail to open the gate to the left tower, the plunger is pushed home, and the screw is retightened so the paddle rests as close to the left flat rail as possible without touching it. Printed switch 42 Inner Loop and the orbit switches 20, 21, 37 and 38 read balls through the loop.
- **Vertical up-kickers and top saucer.** Three vertical up-kickers and one saucer return balls to play. Printed solenoid 3 with switch 9 is the left VUK, printed solenoid 4 with opto 41 is the top VUK, printed solenoid 5 with switch 30 is the right VUK, and printed solenoid 19 with switch 46 is the top saucer. The retained VPW and JPSalas scripts label solenoid 4 Upper-Left VUK and solenoid 19 Upper-Right Kicker; the manual's Top VUK and Top Saucer are the printed names and control.
- **Mini playfield.** A separate mini playfield carries four switches printed Mini PF U.L., U.R., L.L. and L.R. at addresses 33 to 36, all switch part 180-5057-00. The printed switch-location drawing shows the mini playfield moved off the main playfield for clarity, so its printed drawing position is not its installed position; the coordinates here come from the retained recreations.
- **UK-only up/down posts.** UK cabinets add three up and down posts driven from a UK 3X Transformer Driver Board and exposed on Whitestar auxiliary outputs 33, 34 and 35. The cabinet wiring page names them the Post Save feature: the left of the two UK-only cabinet buttons (printed switch 1) raises the Left Outlane Ball Deflector (Aux 1), the right button (switch 8) the Right Outlane Ball Deflector (Aux 3), and both together the Center Up/Down Post between the flippers (Aux 2). The printed coil and flash lamp location drawing prints Aux 1 and Aux 3 at the tops of the outlanes and Aux 2 between the flippers. These are absent from non-UK cabinets, as are the two cabinet-side buttons.
- **On the Limited Edition** the One Ring magnet's own fuse is numbered F7 on the 520-5249-00 board, 4A, which the LE owner's annotated page 101 says "*May need 5A S.B."; F20 is the 2003 board's number.

## How coordinates were resolved

Every one of the 216 placements is validated, each in one recorded way (69 by drawing measurement, 15 by projection confirmed by drawing, 31 by table object confirmed by drawing, 101 by three table agreement):

- **Three-table agreement.** Three factory-layout recreations are treated as three measurements; a lamp, switch or flasher placement is validated on their word alone only when **all three agree** within 0.025 normalized units, because independence between them is unestablished and the two available signals disagree. Measured, the JPSalas and Hanibal tables agree with each other to within 0.0155 on every lamp both model while either differs from VPW by up to 0.187; documented, VPW credits an "EBIsLit - Baseline playfield scan" and JPSalas records its playfield as "based on Ebislit's playfield", while Hanibal names no baseline.
- **A table object confirmed by its own factory callout.** Everything else a table models is checked with `tools/drawing_callouts.py` against the reissue's drawings: the switch drawing DR.4, the lamp drawing DR.5 and the coil and flash lamp drawing DR.7, and for the below-playfield coils the terminal-strip bottom view (PDF 123), which draws every coil assembly in place. Controls are read on each drawing's own line art (bumper caps, flasher domes, round inserts, rollover slots and actuators) and paired with validated placements: pdf-6 8 controls, rms 0.0024, 34 callouts checked; pdf-7 8 controls, rms 0.0033, 68 callouts checked; pdf-9 9 controls, rms 0.0028, 12 callouts checked; pdf-83 11 controls, rms 0.0023, measurements only; pdf-84 6 controls, rms 0.0006, 8 callouts checked; pdf-121 8 controls, rms 0.0008, measurements only; pdf-123 9 controls, rms 0.0009, 44 callouts checked. A placement agrees when its own callout lands within 0.07 under both the control fit and the leave-one-out callout fit; all 166 checks agree. Two switches follow the drawing rather than the tables' own consensus: 28 Balrog Hit takes Hanibal's object, which the drawing prints its box on, and 41 Top VUK takes VPW's.
- **A projection onto a mechanism, confirmed by a drawing.** A coil sits at the mechanism it drives: the VUK and saucer kickers, the bumpers, the trough's eject end, the One Ring under its flasher, and for the right tower, orbit pin, sword lock, Balrog motor and auto launch the object the known-working VPW script moves for that coil. The stacking opto 15 shares switch 14's plan position: both beams belong to one dual opto board pair at the trough's eject end, one above the other. Each is validated only because a factory drawing prints its own callout within 0.07 of it: DR.4 for 15; DR.7 for 6; the bottom view (PDF 123) for Q01, Q02, Q03, Q04, Q05, Q07, Q09, Q10, Q11, Q13, Q19, Q21, Q22.
- **A measurement on the factory drawing that draws the device.** Where no retained table models a device where the factory puts it, the device is measured on the drawing that draws it and, where one exists, checked on a second drawing: Balrog Motor Relay on DR.7, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Left Flipper on DR.7, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Left Slingshot on DR.7, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Loop Diverter on DR.7, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Right Flipper on DR.7, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Right Slingshot on DR.7, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Center Up/down Post (UK) on DR.7, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Left Up/down Post (UK) on DR.7; Right Up/down Post (UK) on DR.7; Left Flipper End-of-Stroke switch on the bottom view (PDF 123), checked on the below-playfield parts drawing (PDF 84); Right Ramp Made switch on DR.4; Balrog Open switch on the bottom view (PDF 123); Balrog Closed switch on the bottom view (PDF 123); Left Slingshot switch (both contacts) on DR.4, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Right Slingshot switch (both contacts) on DR.4, checked on the bottom view (PDF 123); Right Flipper End-of-Stroke switch on the bottom view (PDF 123), checked on the below-playfield parts drawing (PDF 84); the nineteen LED windows on DR.5; the 22 G.I. sockets on the G.I. wiring page (PDF 121), 20 of them checked against the G.I. labels of PDF 123, which prints none at the other 2; the eight back-panel G.I. sockets along the back panel's rear-view inset on the same page, each checked on the panel's exploded front view (PDF 112); and the two return-lane spotlights at the light-reflector leaders of the playfield parts drawing (PDF 83). Switch 24 is the notable one: the ramp parts drawing mounts the Right Ramp Made switch on the sword ramp, where DR.4 prints it, while all three tables register the shot at the right ramp's exit.

The VPW table identifies itself as **"Lord Of The Rings - Valinor Edition"** with original artwork, so where its insert layout departs from the printed diagrams that is an artwork difference rather than an error. Its Fellowship inserts form a closed ring of runes rather than the printed arc of named characters, which accounts for the lamps where VPW alone is the outlier (6, 9-17); DR.5's callouts confirm the JPSalas and Hanibal positions for all of them.

Public flasher 25 drives three bulbs, one in each pop-bumper cap; all three retained scripts bind all three, and every emitter is validated by all three tables. The back-panel inserts 60-63 stand two above two on the upright back panel, so each column's upper and lower bulbs share one plan position, the panel's plane, as the back-panel mode lamps 73-78 do.

## General illumination

One relay channel, four fused circuits. The reissue's G.I. wiring diagram (PDF 121) draws its sockets as circles in each circuit's colour on a bottom view of the playfield, measured through the lane switches' rollover actuators: 7 yellow on the lower playfield, 5 green on the upper middle playfield and 10 violet on the upper playfield, plus 8 brown in a rear-view inset of the back panel. ## Recreation guidance

Bind the flippers once, at 46 and 48. Enumerate the dedicated switches at their negative and 81-84 addresses rather than folding them into the matrix. Treat G.I. as one channel lighting every G.I. bulb at once. Model the trough with the printed numbering, remembering that printed Trough #1 is furthest from the shooter lane, and assert the stacking opto 15 only while a ball blocks it. Drive Balrog Hit from a collision with the Balrog's front hit plate, not from the toy's position. The 8x10 matrix occupies 1-80 and the nineteen LEDs 81-99 on the same lamp transport; bind each LED by the table above, not by an object's number, and do not invent anything at 100-104, which is allocation space rather than physical hardware. On the Limited Edition, bind the right Helm's Deep flasher at 28, leave 14 unbound, and treat 12 as an optional cabinet shaker.
