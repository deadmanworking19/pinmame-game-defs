# Dr. Dude And His Excellent Ray (Bally, 1990)

Coverage: **partial**. Production semantics and hardware have been curated; specific spatial measurements and System 11C prototype fitment remain open.

The production machine is model 2016, released on August 29, 1990 under the Bally name by Midway. IPDB 737 and the factory manuals agree. This record contains the four System 11C firmware sets: `dd_l2`, `dd_lu1`, `dd_l3c` and `dd_p6`. The `dd_p06` and `dd_p7` drivers belong to the separate WPC hardware record. WPC was a documented small physical production batch; its firmware requires different hardware from a System 11C CPU. OPDB `GRVEJ-MDq1w` remains the production identity. The WPC record does not duplicate that resolved claimant.

## Controller and complete I/O

Pinned PinMAME `46e1a01a` uses `GEN_S11C`, `S11_MUXSW2`, `muxSol=12` and `FLIP_SWNO(58,57)`. The record enumerates 64 matrix switches, four service inputs, eight flipper-column bits, the Country jumper, 64 matrix lamps and 50 public solenoid slots. Every slot has a disposition. This includes physically unfitted outputs 5, 8, 9 and 22 that the diagnostic test still pulses, source-proven holes, sound-overlay gaps, virtual GAME ON at 23 and synthetic flipper outputs 45-48. There is no dedicated GI channel: solenoid 10 interrupts playfield GI, and the script inverts that state for its visuals. A/C Select at 12 routes the eight transistors between A-side outputs 1-8 and C-side outputs 25-32; its state is copied into virtual switch 2. The manual's 'Not Used' switch cell does not make that meaningful emulator state unused.

Drive the cabinet flipper buttons at 82 (right) and 84 (left). The core copies them to matrix switches 57 and 58 and fabricates outputs 45/46 (right) and 47/48 (left). Direct writes to 57/58 are overwritten. Physical switches 57/58 are backbox optotransistors. The table's key handlers write the copied contacts redundantly, while `S11.vbs` supplies the effective button bits. The other flipper-column bits at 81-88 cannot reach this System 11 row reader. The matrix contacts are normally open: the direct matrix read, zero inversion mask and active-at-1 script/ROM convention establish that construction. Opto construction at 21-24 and 57/58 is distinct from the matrix contact's rest state.

LA-2 and PA-6 each booted from empty NVRAM and were initialized once. Every subsequent evidence run inherited only that set's hashed initial NVRAM file. All 30 coil selectors, 64 single-lamp selectors and 72 held-switch display readings matched. These tests establish firmware names and address behavior; physical quantities come from the hardware evidence. GAME ON at 23 rose after Start, dropped on tilt and returned for the next ball. LU-1 and LA-3C share the same CPU generation, game data, display and sound ROMs and represent firmware, language or competition revisions. The equipment fitted to an actual PA-6 prototype remains unverified.

## Ball paths and mechanisms

The game starts with three balls in the front trough. Drain switch 10 precedes the transfer by outhole coil 1 into the trough monitored by switches 11-13; feeder coil 2 then sends a ball to manual shooter lane 9. A strong plunge follows right loop 59 to I Test target 49 and the bumpers. Weak plunges return to 9. The script's immediate drain-to-stack transfer and pulses simplify the physical transit. Keep occupancy until the ball moves; an outhole coil does not directly close a trough contact.

The Excellent Ray / Bag of Tricks popper at the top left uses switch 51 and coil 3 and returns the ball through the left habitrail. The Gift of Gab popper uses switch 32 and coil 4 and returns it through the right habitrail. Both are spring-return vertical lifters with no separate position sensor. The retained script's switch release at timer step 7 and `velz=90` are table tuning. Clear occupancy when the ball leaves. A contact that remains occupied can provoke ball search; it does not imply another sensor.

The four right drop targets, 21 at the top through 24 at the bottom, stay down and active until coil 7 resets them. The C-13450 opto board reports each target separately. The table also handles simultaneous neighboring drops. Magnetic Personality has standup target 50 and a separate field coil at 13. That target does not sense magnetic presence and must not be written by the coil. A held ball rolls back toward the left flipper when released.

The left ramp crosses switches 15 and 16 and delivers the ball into the elevated Molecular Mixmaster. Its fixed mini-playfield has nine independent standups around three sides, a motor-driven gear/post that scatters the ball and an open front exit to the right habitrail. Motor output 16 has no home or limit feedback; the motor runs while asserted and coasts off. Preserve nine independent contacts rather than the PinMAME simulator's scripted hit sequence. Spin-up, spin-down and angular physics remain table tuning. Three #44 dome lamps at 33-35 are separate from the six flash bulbs driven by 25-27: three #906 corner sockets and three #89 gear-assembly sockets.

Big Shot switch 31 is a ball target. Coil 14 drives the character through a plunger, link and crank with a return spring. The assembly specifies AE-26-1500 and documents no position sensor. A jam must not be represented by an invented home switch. The table's initial rocking and damped shake provide visual guidance. The two cabinet-wired flippers, three pop bumpers and two slingshots use conventional mechanisms; each sling address covers two physical contacts. Flipper end-of-stroke construction belongs to the winding circuit rather than fabricated ROM feedback.

The factory shot guide collects the Gift of Gab, Heart of Rock 'n Roll and Magnetic Personality ingredients, then the Mixmaster, then the lit Excellent Ray to start multiball and advance the Dude-O-Meter. Mixmaster jackpot and Gazillion targets, the Reflex sequence, I Test awards and the Big Shot animation provide rules and visual context; the ROM remains the rules authority. The retained table's cheater-post, color and ray-beam options are virtual additions, not factory variants.

## Evidence decisions

Complete switch, lamp and solenoid tables and parts lists are transcribed beside the definition. They retain blank cells, connector pins, transistor designators, literal colors and notes. Assembly-specific coil parts take precedence where generic tables disagree. The paginated scan's page 2-42 prints four playfield bulbs for 01C. Its page 1-36, the live Operations Manual's page 2-42, the mini-playfield and gear assemblies, and the ROM's '2 F.L.' labels support two per Mixer circuit. The lower 01C balloon on page 2-43 does not establish additional apron bulbs. The discrepant scan cell is retained in a note.

The lamp drawing repeats labels 22/23/24 near Big Shot. The retained middle and lower playfield photos and script-controlled geometry show one actual bonus ladder; the duplicate labels are a drawing defect. The trough parts list names sides from an underside view; in player view, switch 11 is at the right/feed end. The location drawing also misnumbers the return lanes. The matrix, parts list and ROM identify 19 as Right Return and 20 as Left Return, while the script writes those addresses from the opposite lane objects. The definition follows the physical sides and records the drawing and table defects.

## Spatial limits and completion work

Coordinates use the retained table bounds `(0,0,952,2100)`: x runs from 0 at the left to 1 at the right; y runs from 0 at the rear to 1 at the apron. The repository helper rounds normalized coordinates to six decimals. Primitive centers come from world-space OBJ bounds rather than stored offsets. Actual script bindings select the geometry. The spatial report records every anchor and projection; broad glows, reflections and sprites are not socket measurements.

- GI output 10: the retained views do not establish the factory socket count or complete switched-string membership. The 32 modeled bulb candidates do not prove completeness. Bumper-cap bulbs and any under-apron sockets need reconciliation with the wired layout.
- Flashers 15 and 25-31: the retained lighting helpers do not locate every physical bulb. Each Mixer circuit at 25-27 has a #906 mini-playfield socket and a #89 gear socket. Both remain in its quantity, while unverified coordinates, especially under the disk, remain omitted.
- Lamps 1-5 and 9-12: rear-panel sprites and windows do not locate the individual sockets. A measured elevation or socket model is still needed.
- Sling switches 55/56: each address covers two physical contacts. A single table sling event does not locate both contacts.
- Insert, dome and hidden-coil projections have explicit roles. They need reconciliation with physical drawings or measurements before spatial validation.
- PA-6 agrees with LA-2 in complete service-test sweeps, but those runs do not establish prototype equipment.

Completion requires a wired GI inventory, physical flasher and paired-contact centers, rear-panel elevations for the nine window lamps, checks of the projected inserts and hidden coils, and construction evidence for the actual System 11C prototype. Promotion remains refused until those gaps are resolved. ROMs, complete manuals, community tables and original photos remain outside Git.
