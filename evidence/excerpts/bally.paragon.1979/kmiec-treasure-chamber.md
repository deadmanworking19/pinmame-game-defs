# Paragon - Greg Kmiec on the Treasure Chamber saucer

Source: `Bally_1979_Paragon_Greg_Kmiec_Discusses_Treasure_Chamber_Design.pdf`, the two-page IPDB file for machine 1755 that reproduces an e-mail exchange between IPDB's Jay Stafford (26 October 2012) and Paragon's designer Greg Kmiec (reply dated 27 October 2012). The PDF has a text layer; the passages below were taken from it and checked against the rendered page. Only the designer's reply is quoted, and only the sentences about the saucer's construction.

> The saucer was always in the position that you see. It was always meant to kick up towards the top of the playfield. The thick clear plastic piece on the left side of the channel, that protrudes into the ball lane, directs the pinball into the metal deadening piece on the right side. When the ball hits the metal deadening piece, it loses momentum and falls into the saucer.
>
> What that person might be referring to is that the original whitewood had a straight shot through the ball lane into the saucer. The ball lane had to be somewhat wide to allow a smooth shot to the saucer. Many times, the pinball would be going too fast and jump the saucer and proceed to the top of the playfield, thus negating the Treasure Chest feature. (The shot was too smooth.) The thick clear plastic on the left of the ball channel, that protrudes into the ball lane and directs the pinball into the metal deadening piece on the right, as well as the deadening metal piece itself, were added.
>
> The clear area to the right of the saucer on the "top" plastic was always there. It allowed the player to see the Treasure Chest saucer when standing at the foot of the game (normal playing position.)
